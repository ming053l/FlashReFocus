Abstract

Single-image refocusing changes the focal plane and depth of field after capture. It is inherently interactive: users try many focal points and blur strengths on one photograph, so each edit should be fast and change only the blur. Recent generative methods instead rerun large diffusion models for every edit. Yet refocusing is asymmetric: deblurring must restore lost information, whereas rendering is determined by depth and the focal plane. We therefore propose FlashReFocus, which spends diffusion once on restoration and renders every edit with an explicit, lightweight network. For restoration, a one-step diffusion deblurrer is paired with a decoder refiner that reinjects input structure lost in VAE decoding. For rendering, defocus-bias attention relates pixels by defocus rather than image distance under a depth-derived defocus map, so the focal point and blur strength are controlled directly and in-focus content stays untouched. Editing this map further yields tilt-shift and two focal planes without retraining. We also introduce 3CReal, a three-camera benchmark of 89 pairs. FlashReFocus achieves the best perceptual fidelity for zero-shot bokeh rendering on EBB! and 3CReal and refocuses 148.3× faster than GenRefocus, at about half a second per further edit.

Method

Overview. (A) Stage I restores an all-in-focus image in one step with a frozen latent diffusion U-Net and an optional decoder refiner; (B) the weight ω mixes the pixel and detail LoRA passes to trade fidelity for detail. (C) Stage II computes a defocus map from monocular depth and the selected focal point, and (D) renders bokeh in a single feed-forward pass with (E) defocus-bias attention.

Decoder refiner. VAE decoding loses fine structure of the input. The refiner reinjects multi-scale encoder features and a high-frequency map of the blurry input into each decoder block, which recovers edges and text without another diffusion pass.

Defocus-aware renderer. Depth and the focal point give a signed defocus map; with the blur strength it conditions a residual network. Its defocus-bias attention relates pixels by how similarly defocused they are, not by how close they are in the image, so the renderer adds blur and leaves focused content as restored.

Aperture as a dial

With the focal point fixed, the strength behaves like an aperture: the background blur grows while the subject stays sharp. Strength controllability (LVCorr) is 0.857 on LFDOF and 0.839 on 3CReal, against 0.690 and 0.666 for GenRefocus. The renderer is trained for strengths 0 to 1; values from 1 to 2 extrapolate past training.

Two focal planes

A physical lens has one focal plane. Because the renderer reads an explicit depth map, FlashReFocus can keep two subjects at different depths sharp while the space around them stays blurred: each pixel is blurred by its distance to the nearer of the two planes, with no retraining.

Related work

FlashReFocus builds on and compares with these works on refocusing and bokeh rendering.

  • [arXiv 2025] Generative Refocusing: Flexible Defocus Control from a Single Image

    Two-stage refocusing that restores and then renders bokeh with diffusion transformers.

  • [SIGGRAPH Asia 2025] DiffCamera: Arbitrary Refocusing on Images

    End-to-end refocusing with a single diffusion transformer.

  • [CVPR 2026] MagicBokeh: Towards Photorealistic and Efficient Bokeh Rendering via Diffusion Framework

    Restoration and bokeh rendering fused into one diffusion step.

  • [ICCV 2025] Bokehlicious: Photorealistic Bokeh Rendering with Controllable Apertures
